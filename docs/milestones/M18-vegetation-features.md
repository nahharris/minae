# M18 — Trees and bushes

**Status:** 📋 Planned
**Depends on:** [M17](M17-plains-terrain.md)

## Objective

Scatter trees and bushes across the plains. The first content that is placed
*on* terrain rather than being terrain, and the first thing that makes the world
look inhabited.

Needs two new blocks — wood exists; leaves do not — and leaves are the first
block that wants transparency, which the vertex alpha channel was freed for
back in M4.

## The problem features have that terrain does not

Terrain is a pure function of position: each column is decided independently, so
chunks agree at their seams automatically.

**Features are not.** A tree rooted at the edge of one chunk has branches in the
next. Two things follow, and both are the classic way feature placement goes
wrong:

- **A chunk cannot be generated by looking only at itself.** It must also
  consider features rooted in neighbouring chunks whose extent reaches into it.
  The standard answer is to give every feature a bounded radius, and when
  generating a chunk, evaluate the features of every chunk within that radius —
  writing only the parts that land inside.
- **Placement must not depend on generation order.** Whether a tree exists, and
  where, must be derived from `(seed, chunkCoord)` alone. Deriving it from a
  running RNG, or from whether a neighbour happens to be loaded, makes the world
  depend on the path the player walked — and under the M14 worker pool, on
  thread scheduling.

Both are covered by criteria below, because both produce worlds that look fine
in a screenshot and are subtly broken.

## Placement

A per-chunk deterministic RNG seeded from `(worldSeed, chunkX, chunkZ)` gives a
candidate set; candidates are rejected where the ground is unsuitable or another
feature is too close.

Poisson-disk-style rejection gives noticeably better spacing than pure random
scatter, which clumps in a way that reads as artificial. Worth doing, and cheap
at this density.

## Leaves and transparency

Leaves are the first non-opaque block, which touches several systems that have
been waiting for exactly this:

- **Lighting** — `isTransparent` in the light engine is currently `block == nil`,
  documented as the one place to extend when translucent blocks arrive. Leaves
  should let light through, probably attenuated.
- **Ambient occlusion** — `occludesCorner` asks about shape; leaves should
  occlude weakly or not at all, or every tree will have a hard black underside.
- **Face culling** — two adjacent leaf blocks should not draw the faces between
  them, or a tree's interior becomes a mass of hidden geometry.
- **Vertex alpha** — freed in M4 specifically for this and still a constant 255.

That is a lot of surface area, and it is the reason this milestone is separate
from terrain rather than folded into it.

## Validation criteria

1. **Deterministic placement.** The same seed puts the same trees in the same
   places, always.
2. **Independent of generation order.** A chunk generated in isolation is
   identical to the same chunk generated after its neighbours. The load-bearing
   criterion: this is what makes features safe under the worker pool.
3. **Trees cross chunk boundaries intact.** A tree rooted near a seam has its
   full canopy, with no clipped branches and no half-tree. Test with the root
   in each of the four boundary positions, since an implementation can easily
   handle one axis and not the other.
4. **Nothing floats.** Every tree and bush is rooted on solid ground; none is
   left hanging by terrain that sloped away.
5. **Features do not overlap** into each other, and respect the minimum spacing.
6. **Density is plausible** — assert a range of trees per chunk over many seeds.
   Without it, a change to the noise can silently turn plains into forest or
   empty steppe.
7. **Leaves are lit sensibly**: light passes through, a tree's interior is not
   pitch black, and the underside is not a hard black disc.
8. **Interior leaf faces are culled**, checked on vertex count rather than by
   eye.

## Manual verification

- [ ] Trees look rooted, not dropped on.
- [ ] Spacing looks natural rather than gridded or clumped.
- [ ] No trees clipped at chunk boundaries — walk a long way and watch the
      seams, which is where this shows.
- [ ] Canopies read as foliage rather than as solid blocks.

## Explicitly out of scope

Tall grass and flowers, saplings and growth, leaf decay, biome-varied tree
species, and anything fantastical.
